## 1. The "accuracy rockets to 100%" effect — solved, and it's a real bug

Your instinct was that this reflects the short longitudinal scope. That's
half right, but the actual mechanism is narrower, more specific, and
**fixable now** — it isn't something that just dissolves as data accumulates.

### What's happening

Every single lead-bucket scoring ≥95% contains **zero snow**. Not "mostly
dry" — literally every actual in the bucket is 0.0 cm. There are 23 such
buckets and there are no exceptions:

```
yrno_cal          am lead=4  pct=100.0  n=15  mean_actual=0.00  frac_dry=100%
openmeteo_cal     am lead=4  pct=100.0  n=12  mean_actual=0.00  frac_dry=100%
janesweather_cal  am lead=5  pct=100.0  n=15  mean_actual=0.00  frac_dry=100%
...
```

The skill formula is `100 * max(0, 1 - MAE / mean(max(actual, FLOOR)))`.
When every actual in a bucket is 0, the denominator collapses to `FLOOR_CM`
(2.0) and a forecast of ~0 gives `MAE ≈ 0` → **exactly 100%**. The source
isn't forecasting well; it is being credited for correctly predicting
nothing during a dry spell.

The gradient across all buckets confirms it:

| reported accuracy | mean actual snow | % dry days | buckets |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0–19%   | 2.07 cm | 64% | 35 |
| 20–39%  | 2.15 cm | 61% | 33 |
| 40–59%  | 1.75 cm | 68% | 55 |
| 60–79%  | 1.37 cm | 73% | 98 |
| **80–99%** | **0.14 cm** | **97%** | 30 |

Accuracy in this project is currently, to a first approximation, a
measurement of how dry the scoring window was.

### Why it correlates with lead time (the threshold you noticed)

This is the confounder, and it's the interesting part. Each lead bucket
scores a **different set of target dates**. As lead increases, the window of
dates a source can be scored against *slides forward*. With only ~20 days of
actuals, those windows are 3–4 days wide and barely overlap:

```
janesweather_cal  am lead=3  window=2026-07-14..07-16  actuals={0,1,5}    →  0.0%
janesweather_cal  am lead=4  window=2026-07-15..07-17  actuals={0}        → 97.5%
janesweather_cal  am lead=5  window=2026-07-16..07-18  actuals={0}        →100.0%
janesweather_cal  am lead=6  window=2026-07-17..07-19  actuals={0}        →100.0%
janesweather_cal  am lead=9  window=2026-07-20..07-22  actuals={0,2,3,4}  →  0.0%
```

Leads 4–8 land squarely on the 15–21 July dry spell. Leads 3 and 9 straddle
snow. So the curve reads as "accuracy climbs then collapses with lead time"
when what actually changed was **the weather being scored, not the forecast
horizon**. Different leads are not comparing like with like.

### The specific bug: retired series are being displayed

The three sources producing every one of these 100% readings —
`janesweather_cal`, `openmeteo_cal`, `yrno_cal` — are the **pre-2026-07-13
calendar-day series**, retired when those collectors moved to 7am→7am
windows (commits `f8187f6`, `e047525`). They stopped receiving data on
2026-07-13 and are frozen.

`store.py` documents them as *"non-ensemble, undisplayed"* and lists them in
`NON_ENSEMBLE_SOURCES`. But **that constant is never imported by
`dashboard.py`** — it only gates the ensemble, not display. `accuracy_by_lead()`
is called unfiltered at `dashboard.py:1646` and `:1658`, and the retired
series appear **118 times in the published `docs/index.html`**.

So the dashboard is showing dead, superseded series alongside live ones,
scored over a frozen 13-day window — and they are the only things reading
100%. The code does not do what its own comments say it does.

### Impact of excluding them

Dropping the three retired series removes the artifact completely. Highest
live bucket becomes **81.0%** (snowatch, pm lead=5), and **no live source
currently has a bucket that is ≥90% dry**. The retired rows are 7.4% of all
scored pairs (904 of 12,218).

### Recommended fix (not applied — your call)

Small and squarely a bug fix, since it makes the code match documented
intent. Filter the retired series out of the display path in `dashboard.py`:

```python
from store import NON_ENSEMBLE_SOURCES

RETIRED_SOURCES = {"janesweather_cal", "openmeteo_cal", "yrno_cal"}

def _visible(by_lead: dict) -> dict:
    return {s: v for s, v in by_lead.items() if s not in RETIRED_SOURCES}
```

…wrapping both `accuracy_by_lead()` call sites. Then rerun `python dashboard.py`.

### The deeper issue (deliberately NOT fixed — substantive)

Excluding the retired series hides the symptom. The underlying methodology
issue remains and will resurface on live sources as their horizons extend
into dry spells:

1. **Dry windows are unscoreable but score anyway.** A bucket with no snow
   carries no information about forecast skill, but currently returns 100%.
   The `FLOOR_CM` docstring says the floor exists to stop dry runs making
   sources look perfect — it bounds the effect but does not prevent it. An
   all-dry bucket still returns exactly 100%.
   *Options:* suppress buckets below a minimum total observed snow; or report
   accuracy only over days where `actual > 0` or the source forecast > 0.

2. **Lead buckets aren't comparable.** Comparing lead 3 to lead 7 currently
   compares different weather. A like-for-like lead curve should restrict all
   leads to the **intersection** of target dates scoreable at every lead.
   This costs sample size but makes the curve mean what it appears to mean.

3. **`MIN_HEADLINE_N` doesn't cover the lead chart.** Your uncommitted
   `score.py` change (see §3) gates `accuracy()` at n≥5 but **not**
   `accuracy_by_lead()` — which is precisely the function behind the chart
   showing the artifact. Live sources thin out badly at the tail
   (`yrno pm lead=9` is n=15 vs n=70 at short leads), so the far end of the
   curve is noisy even after the retired series are removed.

My read: (1) and (3) are worth doing; (2) is a genuine design decision with a
real cost, and worth sitting on until more season is banked.
